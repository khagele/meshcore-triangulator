Resolving prefixes and finding the best matching region

How this works

Find where a MeshCore node is, from which repeaters heard it.

1

Enter the clues

Prefixes of repeaters that heard the node — 2, 4 or 6 hex, comma-separated. Add :count for how many times it heard (that becomes its weight).

Second-hop prefixes heard it via a relay — a weaker clue, so they're automatically down-weighted.

2

Pick a cluster, then tune it

Choosing a candidate region pulls its proven links and lists the observers. Nothing you change here counts until you hit Apply.

Weights

Raise one to make that observer pull the estimate harder; drop it to nudge a doubtful one aside.

Duplicates

One prefix can match several real nodes. The one with proven links wins, else the one nearest the cluster. That fallback is a guess, not evidence, so it is called out in the cluster status line. The rest are listed unchecked as duplicate prefix so you can swap the pick.

Missing a repeater

Add its prefix to re-include it — it has to already sit inside the locked cluster, and you'll be told if it doesn't.

Proven links

Green lines are confirmed hops. They set each repeater's assumed range, so removing one you don't believe tightens that range.

3

Estimate

Scores the area against the weighted observers and highlights the likeliest spot. Refine with terrain and line-of-sight for a closer look. Change anything above and just estimate again.
